# FARMS per-link drag-coefficient fit: what it is and what it isn't

Reference for [`plot_com_velocity.py --fit_farms_coefficients`](plot_com_velocity.py).
Calibrates FARMS's per-link

```yaml
drag_coefficients: [[cF_x, cF_y, cF_z], [cT_x, cT_y, cT_z]]
```

from a CFD run's link forces, torques, velocities and orientations.

---

## 1. Why 1-D least squares approximates each coefficient

FARMS models one link / one axis $\alpha$ in the URDF (body-fixed) frame as

$$
F_\alpha(t) = k \, s(t), \qquad s(t) = v_\alpha(t)\,|v_\alpha(t)|, \qquad k = \mu \, c^F_\alpha
$$

Given a CFD force history $F(t)$ that was **not** generated by this model, we
ask: what single scalar $k$ makes $k\,s(t)$ reproduce $F(t)$ best in a mean-
square sense?

Minimising

$$
J(k) = \sum_t \bigl(F(t) - k\,s(t)\bigr)^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
k^{*} = \frac{\sum_t F(t)\,s(t)}{\sum_t s(t)^2}
$$

Geometrically this is the projection of the vector $F\in\mathbb{R}^{n_t}$
onto the 1-D subspace spanned by $s\in\mathbb{R}^{n_t}$. $k^{*}$ is the best
possible scalar because any other $k$ leaves residual energy in the
$s$-direction.

**Equivalent physical reading** — $k^{*}$ is the *energy-weighted average*
of the instantaneous ratio $F/s$:

$$
k^{*} = \frac{\sum_t s^2\,(F/s)}{\sum_t s^2}
      = \bigl\langle F(t)/s(t) \bigr\rangle_{s^{2}}
$$

So $k^{*}$ is the pointwise $C_D(t)$ curve averaged with weight
$v^2 |v|^2$ — huge weight at peaks of motion (where the quadratic term
dominates and signal-to-noise is highest), zero weight at zero-crossings
(where $C_D(t)$ blows up because $|v|\to 0$). That is why the LS fit is
stable where the `link_drag_coefficients.png` plot is wild.

$R^2$ tells you how much of the force variance the single scalar explains.
Low $R^2$ = real fluid force has structure that a quadratic resistive term
cannot capture (added mass, shed-vortex history, cross-coupling, thrust).

---

## 2. The coefficients are not universal — they are biased by the trajectory

Three independent reasons:

### (a) Geometric excitation bias

The fit only uses axes that actually move. Pleurodeles undulates in a
horizontal plane, so in each link's URDF frame

$$
\omega_x \approx \omega_y \approx 0, \qquad v_z \approx 0
$$

and the fit leaves $c^T_x, c^T_y, c^F_z$ undetermined (the guards zero
them). To calibrate those axes you need a trajectory with roll / pitch /
vertical excitation — e.g. a turning run, a dive, a pitching manoeuvre.

### (b) Regime bias

$c^F$ absorbs whatever Reynolds-number regime and whatever unsteady
contribution is present in the training data:

- Low-amplitude beat $\Rightarrow$ skin-friction dominates ($\propto v$, linear).
- High-amplitude, high-$\mathrm{Re}$ $\Rightarrow$ pressure drag dominates ($\propto v^2$, quadratic).

A coefficient fit to high-$\mathrm{Re}$ swimming will **over-predict** the
force at rest and **under-predict** it during rapid manoeuvres.

### (c) Structural (model-form) bias

FARMS drag is diagonal, axis-decoupled, and depends only on the
instantaneous velocity. The real fluid force also contains

- **Added mass** $m_{ij}\,\dot v_j$ (dominant at acceleration reversals),
- **History terms** (Basset / Wagner — circulation memory),
- **Cross-coupling** between axes (tail lateral↔axial),
- **Thrust** (requires unsteady vortex shedding and cannot arise from any
  resistive term).

None of these can be represented by $k\,v|v|$ at any $k$. Low $R^2$ on mid-
body links (~0.1–0.2 on the lateral axis in our runs) is the quantitative
signature: about 80 % of the lateral force at those links is not quadratic
drag.

### Practical rule

Calibrate on a trajectory that covers the operating envelope you care
about. Treat $c^F, c^T$ as *regime-averaged effective parameters*, not
material constants. Always report $R^2$ alongside the fitted scalar.

If you ever need a more faithful model the hierarchy is

1. Anisotropic quadratic drag (FARMS today) — ~10 params/link.
2. + Added mass $m_{ij}$ — +10 params, captures acceleration phase.
3. + Linear viscous term $b\,v$ — clean crossover to Stokes at low $\mathrm{Re}$.
4. Full CFD — the truth being calibrated against.

## 3. Operational notes

- **Excitation guard.** Use the amplitude ratio
  $\max_t |v_\alpha| / \max_\beta \max_t |v_\beta|$ (default threshold
  `--excitation_ratio 1e-2`), not the energy ratio, which scales as
  amplitude$^4$ and is far too aggressive on secondary axes.
- **`--r2_min 0`** zeros any axis whose quadratic fit is worse than a
  constant — i.e. the returned scalar would be meaningless.
- **`--fit_t_start 1.0`** discards the initial transient. The ramp-up from
  rest is dominated by added-mass terms the model cannot reproduce and
  biases the fitted $c^F$ toward spuriously small magnitudes.
- **`--farms_viscosity 1.0`** is the correct value. It is the dimensionless
  scale used in FARMS's `arena.water.viscosity` (all stock arena YAMLs use
  `1.0`). Do **not** pass the real kinematic viscosity of water: `compute_force`
  returns $F = \mathrm{viscosity} \cdot c\,v|v|$, so a physical $\mu$ here
  would inflate $c^F$ by $10^6$.
- **Sign convention.** FARMS uses **negative** $c^F$ for dissipative drag.
  Compare to [`drag_swimming/1guilla_swm_pos_fst/animat_config.yaml`](../drag_swimming/1guilla_swm_pos_fst/animat_config.yaml)
  which uses `[-0.1, -4, -4]`. Negative fitted $c^F$ is physically correct.
